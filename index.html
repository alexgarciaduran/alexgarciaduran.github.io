ABOUT

I build and study machine learning models of how neural systems perceive — bridging computational neuroscience and modern deep learning.

My work spans probabilistic graphical models, recurrent neural networks, and biologically constrained vision models, with a recent focus on adversarial robustness: What breaks these networks, and what that reveals about the computations they actually perform.

Currently a PhD researcher at Centre de Recerca Matemàtica (CRM) and Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC) , Barcelona.

PROJECTS

2026
Adversarial robustness of a connectome-constrained vision network
A deep network whose connectivity is fixed by the Drosophila connectome (Lappalainen et al. 2024) modelling the fly's motion-detection circuit, is benchmarked against CNN and optical-flow (RAFT) baselines on collision detection. Using differentiable 3D adversarial textures, we probe whether wiring the brain's actual circuit into the network buys robustness, and find the disruptions caused by adversarial attacks are similar to those produced by classic reverse-phi motion illusions.

Bistable perception reveals a canonical computation in sensory processing
When a stimulus is ambiguous — a rotating cylinder, a Necker cube — perception spontaneously alternates between two interpretations. I model this as inference over a probabilistic graphical model with lateral coupling, implemented as a continuous-time RNN. Lateral interactions play an important role in representing the statistical associations of the world.

2024
Rapid, systematic updating of movement by accumulated decision evidence
Perceptual decisions keep evolving even while the body is already in motion. Combining psychophysics in humans and rats with drift-diffusion modelling, we show that ongoing evidence accumulation continuously re-shapes motor output — a tight, real-time coupling between decision formation and movement. Published in Nature Communications.
